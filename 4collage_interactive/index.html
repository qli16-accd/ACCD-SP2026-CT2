Original Text

"Of all creatures that have life and reason we women are the most unhappy species.

First we must buy a husband at a great price, and then accept him as the master of our bodies.

This is an even greater evil than the first.

And the outcome of our life's striving hangs on this: whether we get a good man or a bad one.

For divorce is not honorable for a woman, nor can she refuse her husband.

A man, when he is tired of the company at home, goes out and relieves his boredom with a friend or companion.

But we women are forced to keep our eyes on one alone.

They say that we live a life free from danger at home while they fight with the spear; but that is foolish.

I would rather stand three times in battle than give birth once."

The Ballad of Mulan

(Anonymous, Northern Dynasties, c. 5th–6th century)

Click, click, and again click, click—
Mulan sits at the door weaving.
You hear not the shuttle's sound,
Only the girl's sighs.

"Tell me, Mulan, what you're thinking?
Tell me, Mulan, what's on your mind?"
"I am not thinking of anything,
Nor is anything on my mind.

Last night I saw the army draft;
The Khan is calling many names.
The army list is posted in twelve scrolls,
And Father's name is on every one.

Father has no grown-up son,
Mulan has no elder brother.
I am willing to buy a horse and saddle;
From now on I will take Father's place in war."

She buys a fine horse in the eastern market,
A saddle and blanket in the western market,
A bridle in the southern market,
And a long whip in the northern market.

At dawn she takes leave of Father and Mother;
At dusk she camps beside the Yellow River.
She no longer hears her parents calling her name;
She only hears the Yellow River's flowing waters.

At dawn she leaves the Yellow River;
At dusk she reaches the Black Mountain.
She no longer hears her parents calling her name;
She only hears the cries of barbarian cavalry in the Yan Mountains.

She travels ten thousand miles to war,
Crossing mountains as if flying.
The northern air carries the cold light of armor;
The wintry moon shines on iron helmets.
Generals die in a hundred battles;
Warriors return after ten years.

After ten years she comes home;
She sees the Son of Heaven.
The Son of Heaven sits in the Bright Hall
And awards her twelve ranks of honor.

The Khan asks what she desires.
"Mulan has no use for a minister's post.
I ask only for a swift camel
To carry me back to my hometown."

Her parents hear she is returning
And go out to meet her at the city wall.
Her elder sister hears she is coming
And dresses herself at the window.
Her little brother hears she is coming
And sharpens his knife to slaughter pig and sheep.

"I open the door to my eastern chamber,
I sit on my bed in the western room.
I take off my wartime robe
And put on my old-time clothes.
Before the window I arrange my hair,
In front of the mirror I fasten yellow flowers."

She goes out to see her comrades;
Her comrades are all astonished.
They marched together twelve long years,
Yet never knew Mulan was a girl.

The male hare runs with bounding steps;
The female hare's eyes are slightly smaller.
When the two run side by side,
Who can tell the female from the male?"
