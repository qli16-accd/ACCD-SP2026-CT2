Digital materiality Documentation

A documentation page for a projection mapping experiment and a gallery of twenty TouchDesigner variations. The project explores how digital images can move from screen-based artifacts into layered physical space.

Course Creative Technology 2
Medium Projection, image, video
Tools TouchDesigner, projector
Projection

Mapping digital texture onto a physical surface.

For the projection part of the project, I used TouchDesigner's Kantan Mapper to project separate video textures onto different planes in the real world. This allowed me to adjust the perspective, position, and scale of each mapped image so the projection could connect multiple physical surfaces.

Process

From procedural image to spatial experience.

My idea was to create a playful scene where a teapot floats in the air and pours bubble tea through several cubes. The projection links the wall, one vertical cube, and two stacked cubes on the table, so the different three-dimensional positions feel connected by the movement of milk tea and pearls.

01

Projector Output

At first, Toggle Output only appeared on the lower-left corner of my laptop screen while the projector still showed the extended desktop. I fixed it in Window Options by changing Monitor from 0 to 1, so TouchDesigner output appeared on the projector for mapping.

02

Kantan Mapping

I used Kantan Mapper in TouchDesigner to match the projected image to several real planes, then controlled each plane's perspective and position.

03

AI Video Sources

I generated separate videos with AI tools: a floating teapot, milk tea flowing from top to bottom, and pearls moving inside bubble tea.

04

Final Adjustment

I mapped the videos onto the wall and cubes, then adjusted the mapping position, texture size, and scale in TouchDesigner until the final projection felt connected.

Documentation

What changed through variation and projection?

20 Variations: The most interesting part of making the TouchDesigner variations was continuously changing the parameters inside each module and watching how the output responded. Small changes could shift the image, color, timing, rhythm, or motion, so the process felt like testing a visual system instead of making one fixed image.

Learning Process: I also realized that understanding the logic of the TouchDesigner file is very important. For example, a music file can be analyzed for drum beat information, and that data can control the scale or color of a 3D model. I still do not fully understand every part of this workflow yet, so I think I need more practice to become fluent with it.
